**Attributes** represent certain statistics that each character has, and determine mainly how good a character is at certain actions.


## Age


A character is considered a child if they are below 16 years and an adult once they reach 16 years. A character can die of old age after reaching 60 years. (If they die of poor health before it, the death reason will be "Natural Causes".) Each starting character will have a number of lifestyle perks unlocked based on its age.

- Once a character reaches the age of 25, they have a 7.5% chance to lose [] **−0.125** health each year. The chance increases by 2.2% each year.

- Once a character reaches the age of 45, they have a 10% chance to lose [] **1** prowess each year. The chance increases by 1.5% each year. A dynasty can prevent this by completing the [](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/File:Kin_legacy_track.png) Kin legacy.


## Skills


Skills represent a character's expertise across various fields and together with traits determine how good or bad a character is at a given task. Each character will be born with a skill level between 0 and 10 in every skill category, partially determined by both parents. Between the ages of 6 and 16 a child can gain up to 4 more skill points spread across all skill categories, once again determined by the skill of both parents. Skill will be further increased or decreased by various traits and modifiers. All skills cap at 100. The five main skills are diplomacy, martial, stewardship, intrigue, and learning. Prowess is a secondary skill that reflects a character's aptitude in personal combat.

## Dread


Dread is a measure of how feared a ruler is by their vassals and courtiers. A character's dread can range from 0 to 100, with [] Nomadic and [] Meritocratic Khanate rulers having a higher cap of 150. Enough dread compared to another character's [boldness](#Boldness) can make characters either intimidated or terrified, allowing most diplomatic actions to succeed despite negative opinion.

Dread is gained by various actions that inspire fear, such as torturing or executing prisoners. However, it is also possible to lose dread through actions showing compassion or weakness, including releasing prisoners and accepting faction demands. Each character additionally has *natural dread* which is the value towards which dread will drift over time (either up or down), at a base rate of 0.5 per month. The lifestyle perk *[Forever Infamous](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Lifestyle#Torturer)* prevents dread from decaying.

Each character has a hidden boldness attribute, which is decided primarily by its [traits](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Traits) (i.e characters with "Brave" trait will never be Intimidated, while those with "Craven" trait will always be Terrified). A character becomes intimidated by characters whose [] dread is 20 above their own boldness and terrified by characters whose dread is 45 above their own boldness. This includes negative boldness, meaning that characters with 0 [] dread can still have 'Intimidated' or 'Terrified' characters if they are below the boldness level required.

## Fertility


*See also: [Family (relation)](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Family_(relation))*


Fertility represents a character's chance to have or cause pregnancy. Married couples and lovers who are a man and a woman, have a monthly chance to get the woman pregnant based on the average fertility of the two partners. A character has a base fertility of 50%, modified by additive modifiers such as traits and artifacts. There is also a multiplicative modifier from age. The age when it starts to decay is increased by 5 if it has the [] Fecund or [] Whole of Body traits (they do not stack), and another 5 from completing the "Blood" Dynasty Legacy.

Concubines and secondary spouses effectively have half fertility. In addition a mother loses 5% fertility for each childbirth. Fertility is reduced by 15% between non-ruler characters.

Fertility only affects the monthly random chance. Laying with a not-infertile, opposite sex partner during an event will always result in pregnancy.

Chance of impregnation monthly is equal to the average of fertility of parents times 4.75. [[3]](#cite_note-3)

Note: Males can have a maximum number of children; 9 living children plus 2 per additional consort or wife (so 15 total with 4 wives). Pregnancies can still happen from events or [](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/File:Icon_scheme_seduce.png) Seduce schemes. Females can have a maximum number of 9 children (again barring an event-driven 10th pregnancy) unless the 9th pregnancy results in twins.


### Pregnancy and Complications


Women will start showing signs of pregnancy a month after they become pregnant and will gain the [] Pregnant trait for the rest of the pregnancy.

When the woman gives birth there is a chance for the child and/or the mother to become ill or die. The chance increases if the mother has less [] Health and decreases if the mother had 2 and especially 4 children, if rite has the Sacred Childbirth core tenet or if the dynasty has the third [](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/File:Kin_legacy_track.png) Kin legacy. Player characters cannot die during childbirth and their chance to become ill is greatly reduced.


### Disease resistance (fertility)


It is possible for fertility penalties to be negated by the [] Disease resistance (fertility) modifier. Currently, only the [Iron Constitution](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Iron_Constitution) perk in the Whole of Body tree provides some.


## Stress


Stress is a measure of a character's mental health and is gained whenever a character responds to an event or decision in a way that conflicts with their [personality traits](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Personality_traits), someone they're close to dies, the character is [imprisoned](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Imprisoned) in a dungeon or is tortured. Serving as a guardian for a ward can also cause stress if the guardian elects to change the child's [personality traits](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Traits#Personality_traits).  Stress ranges from 0 to 400, with higher levels of stress imposing various penalties and making the character vulnerable to mental break events.

### Mental Breaks


A character breaching the threshold of 100, 200, or 300 stress for the first time will immediately suffer a Mental Break. Mental breaks range in severity from level 1 to 3 and grant the option to either [] lose some stress with a choice of gaining one of two traits (a [Coping Mechanism](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Traits#Coping_mechanisms) or worse, see table below) or [] gain additional stress. If a character reaches 400 Stress, it will trigger a level 3 mental break event and they will lose 100 Stress. Mental breaks often times impart negative temporary modifiers to the character at level 2 and level 3. When the mental break causes a change in Personality Traits, one previous Personality Trait will be removed.

Mental break events can only trigger once every 5 years (8 with the [] Mental Resilience lifestyle perk). If a character does not reduce their stress below 100 before this time is up, they will suffer another mental break. All coping mechanism traits bestow grant a stacking [] **+20%** Stress Loss benefit and a related [stress-reducing decision](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Decisions#Coping_actions) on a 3-year cooldown, making it easier to de-stress before the next mental break occurs.

Some perks grant bonuses for stress levels, notably, the "Thriving in Chaos" (Intrigue lifestyle, +4 Martial, +4 Intrigue, and +6 Prowess per stress level) and "Golden Aplomb" (Stewardship Lifestyle, +10% Monthly Income per stress level).

Your character's stress level is indicated by a reddish bar to the left of your character's portrait at the lower left corner of the map screen. The stress of AI characters is not visible.

## Known languages


Known languages are the languages that a character can speak fluently. Speaking the language of a different culture reduces the different culture [] opinion penalties with both characters and counties of that culture in half. Each character will always know the language of their culture and can learn a number of foreign languages via the Learn Language scheme.

Each character has a foreign language limit of 1, which is increased by 1 for every 5 points of [] learning. If a character knows more foreign languages than its foreign language limit allows it has a yearly chance to be forced to choose between forgetting a language or gaining a [] stress level, or two if [] Lazy. The chance increases with every foreign language above the limit.

Children learn new languages more easily than adults.


## Spiritual fulfillment


Spiritual fulfillment is a measure of how at peace a character feels in respect to their faith. The baseline is calculated based on the traits the character has and whether they are considered virtues or sins. Spiritual fulfillment can be gained or lost by acting in ways that the faith considers appropriate, and Personal Tenets can be adopted to offset harmful traits. Spiritual fulfillment ranges from **-100** to **100**.

## Legitimacy


Legitimacy is a measure of a ruler character's right to rule. Legitimacy can be gained by creating titles (gaining titles through usurpation only decreases the former owner's Legitimacy), releasing higher-ranked prisoners with no demands, taking certain decisions, creating legends, holding court, attending activities, having a Senechal and assigning a Court Musician to the *Bolster Legitimacy* task (effectiveness depends on Aptitude), and winning wars, especially against rulers of higher title rank. These gains can be further modified, positively by having the [](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/File:Trait_legend.png) Living Legend or [] Venerated Ancestor traits, or negatively if the character is a [] Bastard or a [] Legitimized Bastard.

Legitimacy can be lost by losing titles, accepting faction demands, taking tyrannical actions, discovered hostile schemes, exposed secrets, excommunication, marrying Lowborn characters (only applies to primary spouse but making a Lowborn secondary spouse the primary one applies the penalty as well), plagues starting in the Domain, disinheriting dynasty members, and losing wars, especially civil wars - losses are also magnified for characters with a [] Disputed Heritage. When a ruler dies their heirs will inherit part of the Legitimacy depending on how close a relative they were to the previous ruler.

Every ruler starts with a certain amount of Legitimacy. This primarily scales with Dynasty's Level of Splendor, as well as [] Court Grandeur if the ruler has a Royal Court. It is also increased by a a high [] Diplomacy skill and having the [] Just, [] Diplomat, [] Born in the Purple and [] Sayyid traits or traits that are considered virtues and reduced by having the [] Inbred trait, traits that are considered sins, or by being a gender that doesn't match the faith's View on Gender doctrine.

Legitimacy is calculated on a scale ranging from 0 to 2800 and goes through 8 levels. How much Legitimacy is required for each level, and how much can be accumulated before excess is wasted, depends on the primary title rank and culture [era](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Era). Each era will further increase the required Legitimacy for each level by half of the Legitimacy required for level 1. Tribal era values are presented in the table. It should be noted that the title rank starts with [] Baron so for example a [] Count will need 60 for Aspiring.

Characters with [] Administrative, [] Celestial, [] Meritocratic, [] Meritocratic Khanate or [] Ritsuryō government will only have Legitimacy if they are independent. Characters with [] Ecclesiastical or [](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/File:Theocracy_government.png) Theocratic government do not have Legitimacy.

## Merit


[](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/File:Merit_rank.png)  [](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/File:Merit_rank.png)The Merit of a character who passed all examinations


| [](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/All_Under_Heaven)
| Available only with the [All Under Heaven](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/All_Under_Heaven) DLC enabled.|


Merit replaces [] Legitimacy for vassals with certain governments. Every such vassal has a **Career Path** and a **Merit Rank**. If their liege has the same government the vassal can only be given titles if their Merit Rank is high enough and if a character dies their titles and any council position will automatically be given to the character who has the same Career Path and highest Merit Rank although the liege can revoke them at any point. A player can change their Career Path via a decision unless they are landed or a councilor.

Vassals who have Merit may have Elder-Disciple relationships with each other, their courtiers or their liege. The Elder must be either the liege or a character with a higher Merit Rank than the liege and the relationship ends if none of these conditions are valid anymore. If both characters are involved in the Dynastic Cycle situation and they are not part of the same political movement the Disciple will have to join the Elder's political movement or pay 100 [] Influence and 20 [] Opinion with the Elder. Each character can have up to 5 Disciples but the limit can be increased. Elders and disciples can ask each other as well as other disciples of the same elder for [] Gold, [](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/File:Appointment_score.png) Candidate Score, an artifact or a men-at-arms regiment. If the character asking is part of a Political Movement they may also ask for a knight, feast, or courtier with the [] [Confucian Education](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Traits#Confucian_Education) trait. If the request is accepted the character will gain between 10 and 160 [] Merit depending on the primary title rank of the asked character.


### Merit Rank


The maximum Merit Rank a character can reach is limited by their currently-passed exams. Passing an exam requires attending another character's [examination](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Activity#Examinations) activity with the [](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/File:Study_hard_intent.png) Take Examination intent and succeeding.

- [](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/File:Activity_local_examination.png) **Provincial Examination** is passed via the Local Examination activity and increases the maximum Merit Rank by **+2** and increases the Exam Score of the following examinations

- [](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/File:Activity_imperial_examination.png) **Metropolitan** Examination is passed via the Capital Examination activity and increases the maximum Merit Rank by **+2**

- [](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/File:Palace_examination.png) **Palace Examination** is passed via the Capital Examination activity by being among the top 10 characters by Exam Score and increases the maximum Merit Rank by **+3**

## Weight


Every character has a hidden weight, which is hidden but can be set manually when creating custom rulers. It ranges from -100% to 100%. If weight is below -50% the character will gain the Malnourished modifier and unlock the Gain Weight decision. If weight is above 50% the character will gain the Obese health penalty and the Lose Weight decision becomes available. Both modifiers give a moderate [] Health penalty.

Twice per year a character's weight will drift by 5% towards the following value. These changes will be decreased if the character has the [] Diligent, [] Temperate, [] Athletic, [] Whole of Body, [] Hale, [] Robust or [] Herculean/Amazonian trait or is young and increased if the character has the [] Delicate, [] Frail or [] Feeble trait or is old.

## Education


Characters up to the age of 6 can be given an Education Focus geared towards one of the skills. This will default to one of the two attributes in their Childhood trait, but a parent can choose it manually with the button at the lower left of their portrait. This can only be done once per child, and not be changed later on. When the character becomes an adult, they will gain an education trait for the matching attribute.

Between the ages of 6 and 16 the child will build up a hidden education score. It has a chance to increase by +2 every birthday (10 chances), plus some additional chances, as detailed below. Once the character reaches the age of 16 they will acquire an education trait based on that score.


| **Score**
| **Education trait level**|

| 0-7
| ★|

| 8-12
| ★★|

| 13-17
| ★★★|

| 18+
| ★★★★|


The chance of the child getting a higher tier education is increased by the guardian's relevant skill plus half of their learning skill, as well as the child or guardian having a positive intelligence trait ([] Quick/[] Shrewd, [] Intelligent, [] Genius). The guardian or child having a negative intelligence trait ([] Slow, [] Stupid, [] Imbecile) will negatively affect their education, as will pushing them to an Education Focus that does not match their Childhood trait. These modifications are further explained and calculated below.

A child of a culture that discovered the [Knighthood innovation](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Innovation#Knighthood), has a Martial education focus, and is of a gender that can be knighted will also be trained as a squire, receiving a Prowess education trait when they come of age *in addition* to a Martial one. For Prowess, the educator's abilities or any other effects that enhance education are irrelevant; only the child's traits matter. Traits that boost Prowess ([] Hale, [] Robust, [] Herculean, [] Strong, [] Giant) will increase the chance of a high-tier Prowess education, while negative Prowess and other sickly traits ([] Delicate, [] Frail, [] Feeble, [] Weak, [] Dwarf, [] Spindly, [] Wheezing, [] Hunchbacked, [] Inbred, [] Bleeder) will diminish it.

If the child has a guardian of a different faith or culture, when given the guardian, it can be specified whether the child should be raised in the parent's or guardian's faith and/or culture. The child also has a higher chance to copy the guardian's personality traits (and also less likely to have the personality trait opposite to that of guardian's). If the guardian has the Witch trait or secret, they will have the option to try converting their wards to witchcraft.

A guardian has [] **+5** towards the child and the ward has [] **+15** opinion towards the guardian.


### Point modifiers


Several factors can provide extra education score modifiers above and beyond those earned via the normal yearly roll:

- The guardian's council having a spouse that is focused on patronage, has a high learning education, high learning skill, or [] Erudite trait, can trigger adding **+1** free point. This can occur happen multiple times over the course of the child's education, and the points are accumulated.

- Having a child educated by a guardian who has a University building in their realm (and paying gold for the privilege) provides a 60% chance of gaining **+2** points and a 40% chance of gaining **+3** points.[[4]](#cite_note-4) The roll will happen on the 16th birthday. The internal flag for attending a university is set on the child immediately and not removed if their guardianship changes, so even if the University holding is lost or the guardian dies, the bonus will still be awarded.

- If your court has the [Court Tutor](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Court#Court_Tutor) position populated when the child comes of age, an additional education roll occurs. The guaranteed minimum bonus of **+2** points is most likely if the Tutor has Terrible, Poor, or Average aptitude, while Good or Excellent aptitude make getting **+3** points more likely. With Excellent aptitude only, there is an approximately 10% chance of the bonus being an entire **+4** points.

- If the child's dynasty has the second level of the "Kin" [Dynasty Legacy](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Dynasty#Dynasty_legacies) unlocked, an additional education roll occurs when the child comes of age, with success (60%) equaling **+3** and failure (40%) equaling **+2** points. In essence, a guaranteed 2 bonus points with a chance of 1 additional.

- Some cultural [traditions](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Traditions) provide small modifications to educational outcomes, for instance:
The "Charismatic" tradition has a 60% chance of adding **+1** point for diplomatic educations only.

- The "Agrarian" tradition applies a penalty of **-1** point for martial educations.


Note that, with the exception of the points earned sporadically via the guardian's spouse, none of these bonuses take into consideration how long the child "logically" would have been under their effect. As long as you have the Court Tutor, Dynasty Legacy, etc. on the day before the child turns 16, they will receive the full and complete benefit when they come of age. On the other side, this also means that if you have a working Court Tutor for the child's entire upbringing, only to fire them the day before the 16th birthday, there will be no bonus whatsoever.

### Prowess trait


Children undergoing a Martial education can also receive a Prowess education trait when they come of age. To qualify, the child's culture or the educator's culture[[5]](#cite_note-5) must have the Knighthood innovation and the child must be of a gender that can become knights. The system that determines which Prowess trait the child gets is completely separate from the other education traits. The perks, spouse or universities don't influence the outcome.

When the child finishes their education, the game creates a temporary score for them, which starts equal to the Martial education level they obtained. Then, the game changes this score depending on the child's physical traits (multiple traits stack):


| **Trait**
| **Prowess Factor**|

| [] Herculean
| +3|

| [] Robust, [] Strong, [] Giant
| +2|

| [] Hale
| +1|

| [] Delicate, [] Spindly, [] Wheezing, [] Hunchbacked, [] Inbred, [] Bleeder
| -1|

| [] Frail, [] Weak, [] Dwarf
| -2|

| [] Feeble
| -3|


Lastly, there's a 25% chance the score increases +2, 25% chance it decreases by -2, and 50% chance it stays unchanged.

The final score is then the child's Prowess education level.
