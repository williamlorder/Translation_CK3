# Duel

> Source: https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Duel
> License: CC BY-SA 3.0 (Paradox Wikis)

This article has been verified for the current PC version (1.20) of the game.

A duel is a combat outside of battle between two characters. Duels can last either until a character gets wounded or until death. Characters cannot duel if they are children, have the  Blind,  Incapable or  Pregnant trait or a disease trait or are both over 70 and have below 10  Prowess. Characters will wear armor during duels, except for Tribal rulers who will fight shirtless.

## Issuing a challenge

Duels can start either as a result of events or if a character issues a challenge, which can only be done once every 5 years. If the recipient refuses the challenge they lose 150  Prestige while the challenger gains 150  Prestige. Acceptance for a duel is determined by the difference in  Prowess as well as the recipient's  Opinion and personality. AI rulers will never challenge someone with 15  Prowess more than them unless their Rationality is very low. Two rulers cannot duel if they are at war with each other.

## Mechanics

Each duel has two values to keep track of:

- Likelihood of Success is a measure of how well a character is doing overall, both offensively and defensively; having a sufficient lead in this at the end of a round results in a victory by skill.
- Risk of Injury is a measure of how overextended a character is; having this sufficiently close to the character’s own Likelihood of Success means a possibility of a loss by mistake if a victory by skill failed to happen that round. The chance of a loss by mistake is equal to the excess Risk of Injury over Likelihood of Success. If both sides would’ve lost by mistake, the attacker, who is generally the character who initiated the duel, wins.
Both of these values accumulate over the duel. All duels last between 2 and 4 rounds. Starting from the third round, victory from either method becomes increasingly more likely round by round. If there is still no victor after the fourth round, then instead the character with higher  Prowess wins by Sudden Death. In case of a tie in  Prowess, a coin flip decides the winner.

During each round, both characters will be given three combat moves to choose one from; the selection is weighted primarily via their   Prowess: higher  Prowess makes better, more efficient moves more likely to show. When chosen, these combat moves will add to both of the tracked values in different configurations, and some of them have additional effects. During a duel, either character can gain or lose Duel Edge, which modifies the  Prowess of a character for the duration of that duel.

AI characters will choose among the available moves based on their personality:

- Characters with high Boldness or Vengefulness will choose the option that offers the best Likelihood of Success
- Characters with low Boldness or Vengefulness will choose the option that offers the least Risk of Injury
- Characters with high Rationality will choose the middle ground
- Characters without high or low Boldness, Vengefulness, or Rationality will choose at random
