# Religion

> Source: https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Religion
> License: CC BY-SA 3.0 (Paradox Wikis)

This article has been verified for the current PC version (1.20) of the game.

Each character will follow a rite. Each rite belongs to a faith and each faith belongs to a religion.

- The religion determines the sins and virtues that are common among all faiths, what holy building can be constructed, as well as the names of the gods it follows.
- The faith determines what are the holy sites, what is the fervor, what military orders can be hired, and who is the head of faith, if one is available. All faiths have a faith hostility between each other. Faiths may be Organized or Unreformed.
- The rite determines what tenets and doctrines are practiced by every character who follows it.

## Divergence

Every faith has a Mainline Rite. If it has a head of faith the Mainline Rite is the rite followed by the head of faith. If it doesn't have a head of faith the Mainline Rite is the rite present in most counties and holy sites. All other rites from the same faith have a Divergence, which is a measure of how much its doctrines and tenets diverge from those of the Mainline Rite. Every 1% Divergence of a rite reduces the  Fervor of the faith each year by -0.02%.

Each faith has a Heretical Threshold, which ranges from 55% to 95% depending on the doctrines of the Mainline Rite. If Divergence is above Heretical Threshold the rite will appear in red in the list of faiths, its borders will flash red on the map, and it can be targeted by the head of faith with the Declare a Rite Heretical or Enact a Bull decisions and the requirements are reduced by 20% if the head of faith has the  Zealous trait. If Divergence reaches 100% the rite will create a new faith. If a head of rite modified the rite to reach 100% Divergence they will gain 100  Piety and 5  Spiritual Fulfillment.

## Fervor

Fervor is a measure of how righteous adherents believe that a faith is and ranges from 0% to 100%. Fervor determines how expensive it is for rulers to create new rites and how likely counties and characters are to convert.

Fervor increases at a rate of +1.5% each year and is reduced by -0.02% for every 1%  Divergence a rite in the same faith has. Fervor grows more slowly for faiths followed in more than 20 counties. New faiths start at 100% fervor, while historical ones start at 50%. A faith without a head of faith will gain +50% Fervor if the head of faith title is created. Fervor is modified by the Holy War casus belli: attackers in such wars lose Fervor while defenders gain it.

If Fervor is above 50% counties of other rites of the same faith are easier to convert to the mainline rite. If Fervor is below 50% counties of the mainline rite are easier to convert to other rites of the same faith.

### Heresy outbreaks

For  Organized faiths every month there is a chance for a Heresy Outbreak to take place. When a Heresy Outbreak takes place, 10-25 counties and at least one  Duke who isn't a head of faith will convert to another faith within the same religion. The number of counties and rulers that will convert increase with lower Fervor. If there is a player-created faith from the same religion nearby, that faith will always be picked. When the Heresy Outbreak occurs, each ruler that embraces the heresy will add 15% fervor to the religion they are defecting from. ## Faith hostility

Each faith has a view of every other faith that depends on their Theism doctrines, whether they have any syncretism tenets, and whether they have any religion-specific doctrines. The  Pluralist doctrine halves the penalties while the  Fundamentalist doctrine doubles them. Holy wars and intermarriages will end if one of the characters converts to a faith that no longer allows them.

## Faith organization

Faith organization describes how organized and coherent each faith is. Faiths can be divided into organized and unreformed, each having advantages and drawbacks. Most Pagan faiths start unreformed and all other faiths except  Kuzarism start organized.

## Virtues and sins

Virtues and sins are traits which a religion, tenet or doctrins considers virtuous or sinful. Characters receive +10 Same Rite Opinion, +10 Zealot Vassal Opinion, and +1 Monthly Piety for each virtue trait they have and they lose -10 Same Faith Opinion, -10 Zealot Vassal Opinion and -1 Monthly Piety for each sin trait they have. Virtue traits also increase they baseline  Spiritual Fulfillment while sin traits decrease it.

Traits that are considered virtues by a character have a green border while traits that are considered sins by a character have a red border. This takes into account the religion and rite of the character who has the traits, not the player character.

A rite can have up to 7 virtues and 7 sins. Each virtue and sin has a weight and if a rite would have more than 7 virtues or sins the the 7 with most weight will be the rite's virtues and sins.

## Religion heads

Every faith has a head of faith and every rite has a head of rite, which are the formal leaders of the faith and rite respectively.

- The head of rite is the only character who can edit the rite, being able to do it once per lifetime, but cannot create a new rite. They are picked in the following order:
Challenger head of faith if one exists and follows the rite
Head of faith if they follow the rite
The holder of the clerical region with most counties that follow the rite
The ruler whose realm has most counties that follow the rite
- Challenger head of faith if one exists and follows the rite
- Head of faith if they follow the rite
- The holder of the clerical region with most counties that follow the rite
- The ruler whose realm has most counties that follow the rite
- The head of faith only exists for faiths that have the  Spiritual or  Temporal head of faith doctrine. It is a title and to create it a character requires control of a Holy Site, 300  Gold and at least a Devoted level of devotion. Heads of faith can be vassals even if they do not own any landed title. If a faith has a head of faith only he or she can sanctify artifacts and the rite of the head of faith is the Mainline Rite of the faith. Heads of faith unlock special decisions and the Instigate Holy War interaction and can also create, remove and change the status of holy sites. If the Empire Titles Restricted by Organized Faith game rule has been enabled then rulers with  Feudal,  Clan or  Tribal government must have positive  Opinion with the head of faith to create  Empire titles.
If the faith has a  Spiritual head of faith then additional requests are possible with a head of faith or head of rite if the character is not at war with them. They are available even if they are outside diplomatic range. For heads of faith the requests are available regardless of diplomatic range. Player heads of faith will not receive these requests more than once every 2 years.

### Excommunication

If the faith has both the  Spiritual head of faith and Central sacraments doctrines then heads of rite can excommunicate characters of the same faith if at least one of the following is true:

- Character is head of faith
- Character is the  Court Chaplain of the target
- Targeted character is a vassal
- Targeted character is in the same clerical region
- Targeted character has a  Court Chaplain who is subordinate
Excommunication costs  Piety that scales with the target's title rank, level of devotion, and whether they have  Ecclesiastical government or not. Excommunicating a ruler gives them the following penalties:

- Excommunicated trait
- -200 Legitimacy
- -5 Spiritual Fulfillment
- -1 Level of Devotion
- -20 Zealot Vassal Opinion for a year
- -10 Same Faith Opinion for a year
- -30 Opinion with the head of rite who excommunicated them
- -30 Opinion with the character who asked the excommunication if there is one
Heads of rite can also remove the  Excommunicated trait, which has the same requirements as excommunicating. This costs 75  Piety and gives 30  Opinion with the ruler whose trait was removed. The targeted ruler will gain -10%  Piety for 10 years but also cannot be excommunicated during that time.

If excommunication is possible rulers can also ask heads of rite to excommunicate another character who is 12 or older, in which case the ruler who asks will pay the  Piety cost. Acceptance depends on the head of rite's opinion of both characters as well as the targeted character's virtue and sin traits. A  Weak Hook can be used to increase acceptance and a  Strong Hook can be used to guarantee acceptance.

Rulers cannot be excommunicated if they pledged to join a Great Holy War or are the head of faith's diarch.

### Challenger heads of faith

Faiths that have a  Spiritual head of faith can have challenger heads of faith. Rulers that don't have  Ecclesiastical government and whose rite doesn't have a  Temporal head of faith can create them via the Declare an Antihead of Faith decision while rulers with  Ecclesiastical government or whose rite has a  Temporal head of faith can become challenger heads of faith via the Proclaim Yourself Antihead of Faith decision. Challenger heads of faith become their rite's head of rite and gain a titular  Kingdom title.

If a challenger head of faith was created through the Declare an Antihead of Faith decision the ruler who took the decision will becomes the sponsor of the challenger head of faith. The head of faith cannot excommunicate the sponsor or any of their vassals. All decisions and interactions of the sponsor or their vassals that would target the head of faith will target the challenger head of faith instead and the challenger head of faith will always accept them.

Both the challenger head of faith and their sponsor unlock the  Challenge Head of Faith casus belli against their head of faith but other rulers unlock the  Depose Challenger Head of Faith casus belli against both of them.

## Find an Adherent

Rites from faiths where none of the rites have any adherents have a button to find adherents, which lasts 5 years. The chance of success range from 10% to 95% and is calculated the following way:

- +2% per  learning
- +15% if both faiths have the same religion
- +15% if rite has the  Pluralist doctrine
- +15% if rite has Accepted or Virtruaous witchcraft
- +10% if dynasty has the first  Erudition legacy
- +5% if both faiths have the same religion group
If successful a new character who follows the dead rite will join the court.

## Conversion

Any character who isn't a head of faith can convert to a different rite at any point by selecting a different faith in-game and clicking on the button to convert. Converting to a different rite requires knowing 50% of its doctrines and tenets if it's part of the same religion and 60% of its doctrines and tenets if it's part of another religion.

Conversion costs  Piety based on the difference between the doctrines of the new rite and the doctrines of the old one. The cost can also be affected by the following:

- −1% per Learning attribute of character
- −15% if old rite has Pluralistic doctrine
- −20% if holding Stonehenge special building in Salisbury
- −25% if both old rite and new rite has Pluralistic doctrine
- −25% if converting to the rite of a concubine
- −35% if converting to an existing rite
- −50% if converting to an existing rite within the same religion
- −50% if converting to the rite of a spouse
- −50% if converting to the rite of an attacker in a Holy War
- −50% if character's dynasty has 3 legacies in the Adventure tree
- −75% if character has the Apostate lifestyle perk
- −150% if the new rite exists in the realm or a neighboring realm and same religious family
- −225% if converting to an organized faith from an unreformed faith
- −225% if converting to the rite of a concubine while old faith is unreformed
- −250% if converting to the rite of a spouse while old faith is unreformed
- −250% if converting to the rite of an attacker in a Holy War while old rite is unreformed
- −250% if the new rite exists in the realm or a neighboring realm and different religious family
- +25% if the new rite's faith is unreformed while having clan government
- +50% if the new rite's faith is unreformed while having feudal government
- +75% if the new rite's faith is unreformed while having republican government
- +100% if the new rite belongs to a different religion within the same religious family
- +200% if the new rite belongs to a different religious family
- +500% if the new rite is unreformed while the old faith is organized
- +500% if the new rite's head of faith is dead
Regardless of modifiers, the cost to convert can never be less than 250  Piety.

## New rites

New rites can be created by rulers by spending a very large amount of  Piety. The more different the new rite's doctrines and tenets are from the old rite, the most  Piety it will cost to change it. Creating a new rite has the following requirements:

- Adult
- At peace
- Can only be done once per lifetime
- New rite has at least one core tenet or doctrine different from current rite
- One of the following:
Independent
 Duke or above
 Ecclesiastical government
- Independent
- Duke or above
- Ecclesiastical government
- Branched Rite Frequency game rule is not set to None
- Depending on the Branched Rite Amount game rule each faith can have one rite for every 35-140 counties that follow it
- If the  By God Alone DLC is installed and faith has the  Theocratic clerical tradition then it also requires the character to have  Ecclesiastical government, a  Clerical Ruler or  Realm Priest puppet, or be the top liege and have  Celestial or  Mandala government
- Heads of rite cannot create a new rite but can modify theirs
- Depending on the Branched Rite Frequency game rule another rite for the same faith may not be created for up to 45 years
Only doctrines and tenets that are known can be selected for new rites unless the character creating the new rite has the  Prophet lifestyle perk. Once a new rite is created, the ruler, their close family and their realm capital will instantly convert. Vassals may convert depending on their  opinion and difference in  learning. New rites inherit the holy sites of the original rite and new faiths can change the status of holy sites for free up to 3 times. If an AI culture head creates a new rite the rite will be named after their culture and religion.

| Available only with the Roads to Power DLC enabled. Landless player characters can create a new rite at any time if they can afford the cost but cannot reform rites and cannot be heads of rite.

| Available only with the Legends of the Dead DLC enabled. Both creating a new rite and reforming one will grant a  Holy Legend seed.

### Reformation

Rites that are unreformed can be reformed by a ruler who controls at least 3 holy sites by spending a large amount of  Piety. Upon reformation, the new rite will keep the name of the old rite while the old rite will be renamed. Cost and divergence mechanics are the same as for creating new rites.

Reforming a rite does not require changing any tenet or doctrine and AI rulers who reform their rite will never do it except possibly for those regarding the head of rite.

| Available only with the Royal Court DLC enabled. Holding court will always include an event to pay a large amount of  Gold to remove the 3 holy sites restriction for reforming the rite if the following requirements are met:

- Level of devotion is at least  Faithful
- At least three of the counties housing the rite's holy sites follow the rite
- At least one of the holy sites is in domain or belongs to a vassal with the same rite
- At least one other independent ruler follows the same rite, has a holy site in their realm, and is not  Zealous or  Rival
- At leans one independent ruler that is a neighbor or shares a seazone and follows a Hostile or Evil reformed rite from a different religion won a war, controls one of the unreformed rite's holy sites, or controls at least 4 counties that follow the unreformed rite
| Available only with the Roads to Power DLC enabled. If an independent ruler with  Administrative government reforms their faith, the State Rite will automatically be changed to the reformed rite.

## College of Cardinals

College of Cardinals is a mechanic available only to the Chalcedonian and Catholic faiths. The College of Cardinals is made up of up to 70 Cardinals, or rulers with  Ecclesiastical government who were appointed as Cardinal. Cardinals are electors for every title held by the pope, receive a titular  Duchy title, and unlock the Endow Masses decision and Cardinal Benefices law. Cardinal titles are destroyed upon succession or if the Cardinal becomes head of faith. At the start of the game there are 8 Cardinals in 867, 20 and 1066 and 40 in 1178.

The head of faith can appoint new Cardinals, rulers with  Ecclesiastical government can also ask the head of faith to appoint them, and rulers without  Ecclesiastical government can ask the head of faith to appoint the religious affairs councilor as a cardinal if they have a  Clerical Ruler or  Realm Priest puppet or their level of devotion is  Devoted Servant or higher. All Cardinals are subordinates of the head of faith, who can remove Cardinals at the cost of 250  Piety. Cardinals have a small cardinal hat next to the title coat of arms on the map.

The head of faith can also spend 50  Piety to give a Cardinal Order unless the Cardinal has the  Excommunicated trait. Cardinal Orders give every county and ruler in the cardinal's clerical region that follows the same faith a modifier for 10 years.

## Canonization

| Available only with the By God Alone DLC enabled. Adult rulers can canonize dead ancestors every 25 years through the Canonize a Venerated Ancestor decision. All religions can do it but  Christianity must not have Veneration of Saints as a prohibited or unknown tenet. Characters can only be canonized if they are dead, followed a rite from the same faith, were part of the same dynasty, had at least one trait that is considered a Virtue by the current rite, and did not have any trait that is considered a Sin by the current rite.

If the faith has a head of faith the said head of faith must accept and acceptance if 33% for each trait the dead ancestor had that is considered a Virtue. The chance is also increased by 33% if the head of faith has the  Cynical trait or if the dead ancestor had Veneration of Saints as a personal tenet. If the head of faith refuses the decision cost will be refunded.

Canonized characters can only be buried in certain places. If rite has  Ecclesiastical rulers the resting place must be the an Ecclesiastical estate, or if all of them have already been used then an adjacent barony. Otherwise the resting place must be a holy site.

Once a dead ancestor is canonized it will gain the  Venerated Ancestor trait and the ruler who took the decision will gain +20  Opinion with every dynasty member for 20 years. If faith had a head of faith who accepted it the head of faith will also gain +10  Opinion with every dynasty member for 20 years.

If a ruler has in their realm the resting place of a canonized character it will have the option to extract relic artifacts. Extracting relics costs 300  Gold and 2000  Piety and the cost increases by 50  Gold and 250  Piety for every canonized character from the same dynasty. The rarity of the artifact depends on how many traits that are considered a Virtue the canonized character had and decreases the more relics are extracted from it. Up to 4 relics can be extracted from the same canonized character and AI rulers will never extract the last relic.

## Monastic orders

| Available only with the By God Alone DLC enabled. Monastic orders are independent organizations devoted to a tenet. Each monastic order has a  Duke rank title. All members of a monastic order have the  Devoted trait.

Holdings leased to a monastic order may be revoked by any ruler in de jure hierarchy whose realm contains the leased holding and is at peace unless the grandmaster has a  Strong Hook on them. Revoking the last leased holding of a monastic order will destroy it. Revoking the lease of a monastic order gives -50  Opinion with the grandmaster and -30  Opinion with the monastic order's head of faith. It will also give the following:

- -1  Development for every level the revoked leased holding had
- -5  Spiritual Fulfillment if the monastic order belongs to a faith considered Righteous, Astray or same faith
- -500  Piety if the monastic order belongs to a faith considered Righteous or same faith
- -100  Piety if the monastic order belongs to a faith considered Astray
- +250  Piety and +20  Opinion with the head of faith if the monastic order belongs to a faith considered Hostile or Evil but the monastic order's faith will also gain 10  Fervor
- If the barony's county had the same faith as the monastic order it will gain -15 Popular Opinion and -0.15 Monthly Control for 10 years
A  King or above who controls the barony where a monastic order is located will be its Patron, even if they are a subject. If the top liege is a  Duke or below the monastic order will have no patron. Patrons of a monastic order may ask the order to change its tenet to one of the patron's personal tenets but the abbot must accept.

An adult head of faith can disband monastic orders of the same faith at the cost 500  Piety, 30  Opinion with every monastic order of the same faith and 75  Opinion with the abbot.

### Creating monastic orders

Creating a monastic order costs 1000  Piety and 500  Gold or  Treasury and has the following requirements:

- Adult
- At peace
- Rite has the Encouraged or Accepted monasticism doctrine
- Duke or above
- Devoted Servant or higher level of devotion
- Not already the Patron of a monastic order
- If the  By God Alone DLC is installed and faith has the  Theocratic clerical tradition then it also requires the character to have  Ecclesiastical government, a  Clerical Ruler Puppet or a  Realm Priest Puppet. Creating a monastic order costs 1000  Piety and 500  Gold or  Treasury.
Creating a monastic order requires an owned county that follows the same faith and has at least one empty barony. The construction of a  Temple holding will begin and when completed it will be leased to the monastic order, the faith will gain +5  Fervor, and the ruler who created it will become its Patron receive the following rewards:

- Can choose which tenet the monastic order will use from all core tenets of the character's rite or the personal tenets of the character
- +3 Spiritual Fulfillment
- +0.5 Monthly Piety for 5 years
- +1 Learning per Level of Devotion for 5 years
- -3% Spiritual Fulfillment Loss for 5 years
- +5 Same Faith Opinion for 5 years
- +20 Opinion for 20 years with the head of faith if there is one
- +10 Tenet Popularity for the monastic order's tenet if religion is Christianity
